Kerosene engines are costly, dirty and wasteful

04 · How it works

From sunshine to sea in four simple steps

1 · CaptureSolar panels on shore turn sunlight into electricity.
2 · StoreA shared battery bank saves it, so boats can charge day or night.
3 · ChargeBoat batteries are topped up at the landing while the crew rests.
4 · SailThe electric motor takes the boat out to fish, quiet and smoke-free.

Pilot first, hub later. During the pilot the boats can charge from the ordinary power supply. The solar energy hub is the next step once the boats have proved themselves.

06 · The pilot

Three boats. Three set-ups. Real fishing.

Each boat tries a different combination side by side in real sea conditions, so we learn what works best before going bigger.

Boat 1
Swappable packs Light battery packs that are lifted off, charged ashore and swapped in the morning.
Boat 2
Extra power A larger motor with spare strength for heavy catch days and rough seas.
Boat 3
Plug-in charging The battery stays on board and is charged by plugging in at the landing.
We compare:RangeEase of useCharging timeRunning costReliability at sea

Set-ups shown are examples of what we will compare. The final three are chosen after measuring the boats.

08 · Timeline

From first boat to rollout in four months

Month 1PrepareMeasure the boats, choose the three set-ups, fit motors and batteries.
Month 2Test at seaThree boats go fishing as usual. Every trip is recorded.
Month 3Listen and refineGather feedback, fix what doesn’t work, improve the design.
Month 4Ready to roll outA refined solution, proven at Manapad, ready for more boats.

No lost fishing days. Crews keep fishing as normal throughout the pilot, and the boat’s second engine stays on board as a backup.
